3 · Title patterns that correlate with more views/day

✓ What the winners do

  • Lead with a provocative thesis, not a description. 4 of the top 8 essays open on a contrarian/emotional claim — “Killing Nazis Isn't Woke”, “The Best Game I Ever Hated”, “Metal Eden Is a Banger and You're Sleeping On It”. Only 1 of the bottom 8 does.
  • Use the “GAME — editorial claim” em-dash format. 4 of the top 8 use an em-dash / “ - ” hook (“The Corporation Is the Monster — Alien: Isolation”); 0 of the bottom 8 do.
  • Name a character or a sharp thesis: “The Guilt of James Sunderland”, “The Corporation Is the Monster” — concrete nouns beat abstractions.

✗ What holds the back catalogue back

  • Format-label suffixes bury the hook. 6 of the bottom 8 carry “|| Intermission NN” or “|| A Video Essay”; only 3 of the top 8 do. These tags mean nothing to a new viewer and eat the first-glance space.
  • Hashtag soup in the title. “The Games I Loved In 2024 || #videoessay #goty #goty2024 #videogames” reads as noise and pushes the actual topic off-screen in the feed.
  • Poetic titles that hide the game. “The Dad Game That Replaced Trauma With Tenderness” never says Pragmata; “Zen & The Art Of Deep Space Mining” buries EVE Online. No searchable keyword = no search traffic.

Rule of thumb for this channel: [Searchable game name] + [contrarian/emotional thesis], ~55–70 characters, em-dash to join them, and no “Intermission NN / A Video Essay / #hashtags” tail.

Method & caveats

This report uses public YouTube Data API v3 signals only — view count, likes, comments, title, tags, thumbnail, publish date and duration. It has no access to CTR, impressions, traffic sources, or audience-retention data, which live behind the YouTube Analytics API (OAuth). Every “this drives performance” statement here is therefore an informed hypothesis from public patterns, not a measured cause — in particular, the normalised metric (views-per-day-since-publish) can't separate a title/thumbnail problem from a topic-demand problem, and a single algorithmic breakout can distort channel averages.

Two specific limits this run: (1) the “views/day” comparison is skewed by the Wolfenstein outlier, so essays are judged against the essays-only median (10.8/day), not the raw average; (2) the sandbox generating this page had no network egress to YouTube's image CDN, so thumbnails could not be downloaded and pixel-inspected — thumbnail critiques are inferred from composition and metrics, and the images are embedded live from YouTube for your own comparison.

Biggest single upgrade available: connecting a YouTube Analytics OAuth credential would replace these hypotheses with real CTR and retention numbers — turning “this title probably hurts discovery” into “this thumbnail gets a 2.1% CTR vs the channel's 4.8% median,” which would sharpen every recommendation above.
