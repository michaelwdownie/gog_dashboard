3 · Title patterns that correlate with better performance

✅ What's working

  • Thesis + dash + subject. Every top essay states an argument, then names the game: "Killing Nazis Isn't Woke — …Wolfenstein" (520K), "You Are The Deep State — …Zero Parades" (10K), "The Corporation Is the Monster — Alien: Isolation" (69 v/day).
  • Contrarian / tension framing beats flat praise. "The Best Game I Ever Hated" (TLOU2, 37K), "Brave Enough to NOT Be Silent Hill 2", "…That Aren't the Obvious Ones."
  • A clear stakes word up front: Guilt, Monster, Redemption, Nazis, Deep State — moral/political weight travels.
  • Curiosity gaps: parentheticals like "(That Aren't the Obvious Ones)" reliably lift the roundup format.
  • Name the game. Every top performer contains a searchable game/series name. The buried essays that hide it (Pragmata as "The Dad Game", "Three Isolated Demos") forfeit all search traffic.

❌ What's dragging

  • The Subject || A Video Essay #tag #tag scaffold. "A Video Essay" and stacked hashtags eat title space and add zero click value (EVE, Games 2024, the Nine Sols intro clips).
  • Series tags exposed to strangers: || Intermission 01/03 means nothing to a non-subscriber scrolling a thumbnail grid.
  • Flat superlatives with no tension: "Is the Best Game FromSoftware Ever Made" — a claim only fans act on.
  • Poetic titles that omit the game name before a video is surfacing: "The Dad Game That Replaced Trauma With Tenderness" hides Pragmata; great once discovered, invisible to search until then.
  • Status-update Shorts ("script is DONE", "Coming this week!", "IYKYK") — fine as community posts, but they dilute the upload feed and average <150 views.

6 · What this analysis can and can't see

This report is built entirely from public metrics exposed by the YouTube Data API v3 — view count, likes, comments, titles, tags, durations and publish dates. It has no access to YouTube Analytics: no click-through rate, no impressions, no average-view-duration or audience-retention, no traffic-source or search-term data. So every claim about why a video over- or under-performs (especially "the thumbnail is the ceiling" or "the title is costing clicks") is an informed hypothesis, not a measured fact — views/day is a proxy for discovery, not a diagnosis of it. Connecting a YouTube Analytics OAuth login would sharpen this dramatically: real CTR would confirm which refreshes to prioritize, impressions would separate "bad thumbnail" from "never surfaced," and retention would tell us whether the long-form format holds viewers or loses them. If that connection becomes available, this dashboard can move from hypotheses to measured levers.
