3Title patterns that correlate with better performance

Across the 18 long-form essays, two patterns separate the winners from the buried:

Argument / provocation in the title ▲ works

Essays that lead with a thesis or contrarian hook average 442 views/day vs 9.6/day for neutral/descriptive titles (excluding shorts). Even setting the viral outlier aside, the top non-viral essays all make a claim:

  • “The Best Game I Ever Hated” — Last of Us II · 36,063
  • “The Corporation Is the Monster” — Alien: Isolation · 4,395 (84/day)
  • “The Guilt of James Sunderland” — Silent Hill 2 · 34,062
  • “Killing Nazis Isn’t Woke” — Wolfenstein · 483,212

“Intermission NN” series tags ▼ hurts

Videos carrying an “Intermission 0X” suffix average 8.6 views/day vs 31.3/day for standalone essays. The internal numbering means nothing to a new viewer and reads as “minor / filler.” The one Intermission that broke out (Metal Eden, 22.6/day) did so despite it, on the strength of “Is a Banger and You’re Sleeping On It.”

Also load the searchable noun early. Titles that hide the game name behind poetry (“Zen & The Art Of Deep Space Mining” → EVE Online) or a vague label (“Three Isolated Demos”) starve themselves of search traffic. Put the franchise the audience types — Silent Hill, Cyberpunk 2077, Armored Core — in the first few words.

What the winning frames share

  • One clear subject, big. The Sunderland / Last of Us / Nine Sols frames read instantly at sidebar size; the “Three Isolated Demos” collage and multi-element frames don’t.
  • Recognizable IP on screen. Winners show a face or key-art the audience already knows. Refresh targets often show generic gameplay or abstract art — nothing to latch onto in half a second.
  • Dark base + a single accent color, which matches the essay tone and makes text pop. Bright, cluttered, or low-contrast frames blend into the feed.
  • No series-number badge. “Intermission 0X” on the thumbnail spends pixels on information that only helps existing subscribers, not the browse/search viewer you need to convert.
